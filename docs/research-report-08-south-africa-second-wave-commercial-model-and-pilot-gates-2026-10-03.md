# SynapseCore Research Report 08 — South Africa Second-Wave Targets, Commercial Model and Pilot Qualification Gates
**Date:** 2026-10-03 (Africa/Johannesburg)  
**Purpose:** Decide the second-wave South African target sequence, define how operational-control solutions are actually bought locally, and establish the commercial/qualification gates SynapseCore should use before proposing a pilot.  
**Important:** This is strategic research, not proof that any named company will buy SynapseCore or that public case-study outcomes will transfer to SynapseCore.

## 2. What South African buying evidence tells us

A 2025 University of Johannesburg study of Digital Control Tower implementations in South Africa found that projects can be acquired through:
- strategic partnerships with service providers;
- formal bidding/tender processes;
- standalone solution purchases.

The study says approval depends on a business case aligned to strategic objectives, executive buy-in, scope definition, funding model, team selection, systems integration, project management and change management. It also found that financial/commercial details and benefit-realisation data are often guarded and that many implementations had weak baseline/KPI definition.

Source:
https://www.scielo.org.za/scielo.php?pid=S1815-74402025000100001&script=sci_arttext&tlng=en

**SynapseCore implication:** a first serious company will likely require more than a product demonstration. It will need a credible business case, sponsor, technical feasibility, defined scope, project owner, baseline metrics and explicit implementation plan.

## 3. Commercial benchmark: Transnova + Tiger Brands

Transnova's public Tiger Brands material shows several important commercial patterns:

- Tiger Brands originally appointed Transnova to transform logistics operations.
- The relationship developed into a centralised control-tower programme spanning people, process, technology and governance.
- Public material describes a **performance-aligned commercial model**.
- The 2026 mandate was re-competed through a formal RFP/open tender and Transnova was reappointed and expanded as lead logistics partner.
- Transnova reports over R300m cumulative transport/efficiency savings, R900m+ annual transport disbursement management and national multi-carrier operations.

Sources:
- https://transnova.co.za/2025/12/05/tiger-brands-and-transnova-win-gold-awardat-the-2025-logistics-achiever-awards/
- https://transnova.co.za/2026/07/20/transnova-expanded-as-tiger-brands-lead-logistics-partner-after-open-tender/
- https://transnova.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Case_Study_-_Tiger_Brands_-_Logistics_Control_Tower.pdf

These are vendor/customer-reported outcomes and should not be used as SynapseCore ROI claims.

### What we learn
1. Serious operational-control spend can be justified when measurable business outcomes exist.
2. Commercial relationships can expand after early value is established.
3. Enterprise customers may require formal procurement/tender processes.
4. Long-term contracts are more defensible when performance is continually measured.
5. Software alone is not the entire delivered product; operating process and specialist support can be part of the commercial proposition.

## 4. Why we should not publish a SynapseCore enterprise price yet

Public South African enterprise-control pricing is sparse because:
- scope varies;
- integration burden varies;
- managed-service intensity varies;
- company scale varies;
- security, implementation and operating support vary;
- commercial terms are often negotiated privately.

Therefore an arbitrary fixed monthly fee would be weak at this stage.

### Better initial structure

#### Phase A — discovery / qualification
Potentially no-cost or founder-funded at first, but time-boxed and selective.

Goal:
- confirm problem;
- identify sponsor;
- map current operating loop;
- identify baseline;
- decide whether a serious pilot is warranted.

#### Phase B — paid controlled pilot
Price should cover:
- implementation effort;
- integration/configuration;
- customer-specific mapping;
- hosting/infrastructure;
- support;
- measurement work;
- risk/contingency.

Pilot fee may be fixed for an agreed scope because both parties need cost certainty.

#### Phase C — production / expansion
Only after value is proven, choose the commercial structure from:
- platform + support/service fee;
- usage/event-linked fee where measurement is credible;
- outcome/performance-linked component where attribution is agreed;
- hybrid base + variable model.

Do not make 100% of compensation outcome-based before we can control attribution. Customer operations, demand, suppliers, staff and external partners can affect the result.

## 5. Plumblink — strongest second-wave target

### Why it matters
Plumblink currently reports:
- 160+ branches;
- 10,000+ products;
- 600+ local/international suppliers;
- national centralised accounts;
- online ordering/stock checking;
- regional warehouse structure.

Sources:
- https://www.plumblink.co.za/about-us
- https://www.plumblink.co.za/companyprofile
- https://www.plumblink.co.za/documents/Plumblink_Company_Profile_2024.pdf

### More important: how Plumblink buys technology
A Netstock case study describes Plumblink's earlier transformation from decentralised stores toward centralised distribution. It reports:
- prior information silos and spreadsheet dependence;
- deliberate selection of best-of-breed solutions integrated to ERP;
- Netstock for inventory and CQuential for warehouse management;
- top-management sponsorship;
- participation by DC managers and other departments;
- a preference for practical demonstrations rather than sales pitches;
- financial ROI expectations;
- reported gains in labour productivity, stock write-offs and stock-take operations.

Source:
https://www.netstock.com/blog/plumblinks-warehouse-and-inventory-management-journey/

### Why this is strategically valuable
Plumblink is not attractive because it lacks systems. It is attractive because it is a mature example of:
**ERP + inventory specialist + WMS + large branch network + supplier complexity + customer service + proven willingness to invest when the operational case is strong.**

That makes it a strong test of SynapseCore's incremental value.

### What we must discover
- What now sits above/between ERP, Netstock, CQuential and branch operations?
- How are project/customer priorities handled across branches/warehouses?
- How are exceptions and cross-domain consequences managed?
- What has changed since the historical Netstock case study?
- Are operational decisions already sufficiently coherent?
- Where would SynapseCore add value rather than duplicate?

### Recommendation
Do not send generic outreach yet. Prepare a targeted approach that acknowledges their mature architecture and asks specifically about cross-system operational control.

## 8. Pilot qualification gates

A company is not pilot-qualified because it is large or complex.

Require all of these categories to be sufficiently strong:

### Gate 1 — real operational problem
Company confirms a meaningful cross-system/cross-team outcome that creates:
- delay;
- manual coordination;
- weak priority;
- poor follow-through;
- stale/uncertain state;
- recovery burden;
- or another measurable consequence.

### Gate 2 — sponsor
A named operational owner agrees the problem matters and will participate.

### Gate 3 — operator
At least one real operator/controller can explain and test the current workflow.

### Gate 4 — data/source feasibility
Approved authoritative sources exist and can be integrated within the pilot timetable.

### Gate 5 — bounded connected scope
Scope exercises more than one operational responsibility, but does not require an enterprise-wide rollout.

### Gate 6 — measurable baseline
Before pilot, define at least one meaningful current-state metric.

### Gate 7 — technical compatibility
The final SynapseCore M6/M7 accepted system can support the necessary lane without pretending unsupported domain actions exist.

### Gate 8 — authority/safety
Roles, approvals, source ownership and prohibited actions are explicit.

### Gate 9 — operational adoption
The company commits operators and time to use the system during the observation period.

### Gate 10 — economics
There is a credible path for the measurable value to exceed implementation and operating cost.

If any critical gate fails, do not force the pilot.

## 9. Customer-1 pilot commercial package I recommend

### Scope
One bounded operation, region, DC/branch set or customer/order flow.

### Duration
Long enough to cover normal operations and real exceptions; do not choose an arbitrary duration until event frequency and cycle time are known.

### Included
- operational discovery;
- source mapping;
- controlled onboarding;
- tenant/operator/authority configuration;
- pilot hosting;
- operator training;
- live observation;
- incident/recovery support;
- baseline and outcome measurement;
- weekly review;
- final value and reliability report.

### Explicit exclusions
- unsupported direct source execution;
- uncontrolled enterprise rollout;
- unverified savings promises;
- autonomous decision authority;
- hidden custom development outside agreed scope.

### Commercial principles
- scope and responsibilities written before start;
- customer contributes real operational staff and source access;
- implementation cost not disguised;
- success criteria agreed before measurement;
- expansion is a separate decision;
- production reliance is not automatic after pilot.

## 10. How the sales process should progress

**Research -> targeted contact -> operational discovery -> source/system discovery -> qualification gates -> pilot design -> commercial proposal -> M7 technical verification -> launch -> measured M8 pilot -> expansion decision**

Do not jump from email reply to demo to contract.

## 12. Current strategic judgement

South Africa is showing a credible market with three simultaneous truths:

1. **The problem exists.**
2. **Companies already spend money and organisational effort solving it.**
3. **Therefore SynapseCore must prove incremental operational/economic value rather than merely prove that the problem exists.**

That is the standard we should maintain.
