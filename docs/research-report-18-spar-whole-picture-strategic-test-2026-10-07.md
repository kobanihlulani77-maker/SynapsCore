## 1. Why SPAR matters

SPAR Southern Africa is structurally different from Shoprite.

Public SPAR material describes:
- more than 2,000 entrepreneurial independent retailers across the Southern African operating model;
- six major regional distribution centres in South Africa, plus specialist distribution operations;
- central wholesale/distribution and buying power;
- digital customer channels including SPAR2U and Uber Eats;
- ongoing ERP/WMS and technology transformation;
- a regional/federated operating model rather than one fully corporate store estate.

Sources:
- https://thespargroup.com/investors/
- https://thespargroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/FY25-Integrated-Annual-Report.pdf

This creates a strong SynapseCore research environment because operational truth and priorities can span central wholesale/distribution, regional DCs, independent retailers, suppliers and digital channels.

## 3. Historical transformation lesson

Earlier SPAR reporting recorded difficulties around one SAP/WMS rollout, including pricing visibility and warehouse-management inefficiencies, followed by a decision to use the best system suited to the business rather than force SAP as the full solution.

Source:
- https://thespargroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/FY24-Interim-results-presentation.pdf

This is historical evidence only; it must not be used to claim SPAR has a current technology problem.

The useful lesson is architectural:

> SPAR itself has demonstrated willingness to use multiple specialist systems and to separate ERP from the total operating solution.

That is compatible with the SynapseCore model of sitting above authoritative source systems rather than replacing them.

## 4. Whole-picture pilot hypothesis

A valid SynapseCore pilot would **not** be:
- an order dashboard;
- inventory analytics;
- a WMS comparison;
- a replacement for SPAR's ERP programme;
- a full-national rollout.

A valid future pilot would choose a meaningful regional distribution/store scope and connect enough authoritative operational data across the relevant supported domains to exercise:

**source truth -> connected operational state -> cross-domain pressure/intelligence -> prioritisation/recommendations -> governed operator decision -> handoff/action -> outcome/readback -> audit/realtime -> replay/recovery**

The selective-data rule means only the required records/fields and agreed footprint are onboarded. It does not mean reducing the product to a single domain.

---

## 5. Why SPAR's independent-retailer model is especially interesting

SPAR's independent retailer structure creates a different governance/control problem from a centrally owned store network.

Research questions include:
1. What operational facts are centrally authoritative versus retailer-owned?
2. Which priorities can SPAR distribution leadership decide centrally?
3. Which actions require retailer agreement or remain local?
4. How are changing DC conditions communicated to independent retailers?
5. How are retailer orders, inventory availability and fulfillment consequences reconciled?
6. How does digital demand affect regional distribution priorities?
7. What happens when a regional/source system is stale or unavailable?
8. How does the central operation verify that the expected business outcome occurred?

This makes authority/governance a first-class discovery issue, not just technology integration.

## 7. Strategic judgement

SPAR is a **high-value strategic discovery account**.

It is not automatically the easiest first pilot because:
- the operating model is federated;
- retailer authority is distributed;
- the national technology transformation is active;
- customer-specific data/role boundaries may be complex.

But those are discovery/implementation factors, not reasons to reject the company.

SPAR is valuable because it tests whether SynapseCore can form a coherent control picture across an operation where central distribution and independent local operators coexist.
