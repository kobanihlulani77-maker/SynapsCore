# SynapseCore Research Report 07 — South Africa Buyer-Side Ownership Map
**Date:** 2026-10-03 (Africa/Johannesburg)  
**Purpose:** Identify which roles inside South African companies already own the operational-control problem SynapseCore addresses, and define the correct founder entry path.  
**Important:** This maps organisational ownership patterns from public evidence. It does not identify a guaranteed buyer, budget holder, or sponsor at any named company.

---

## 1. Executive conclusion

The operational-control problem is already visible inside South African organisations through several role families:

- Control Tower / Network Control
- Order Fulfilment / Customer Fulfilment
- Supply Chain / Distribution Operations
- S&OP / Integrated Business Planning
- Customer Service & Logistics Systems
- Distribution Planning & Analytics
- Supply Chain Analytics / Transformation
- Business Systems / ERP / WMS / OMS / TMS ownership

The strongest first commercial door for SynapseCore is usually **the operational owner accountable for an end-to-end business outcome**, not the IT administrator of one system.

Recommended entry model:

1. **Operational sponsor:** person accountable for distribution, fulfilment, customer service, supply chain or operational performance.
2. **Operational champion:** person who handles exceptions/coordination daily (control tower, fulfilment, planning, branch/DC operations).
3. **Systems/data owner:** business systems / supply chain systems / IT / analytics person who can confirm source architecture and integration.
4. **Domain owners:** procurement, warehouse, transport, commercial/customer-service, production/quality where relevant.
5. **Executive/financial approver:** brought in when business case, risk and commercial terms become material.

Do not reverse this by starting with IT and asking them to invent the business problem.

## 3. Buyer-side role taxonomy for SynapseCore

### A. Executive / economic sponsor
Common titles:
- COO
- Supply Chain Director
- Logistics Director
- Head of Operations
- Head of Customer Service & Logistics
- Head of Fulfilment

Owns:
- service outcomes;
- cost;
- operational risk;
- transformation priority;
- budget / executive sponsorship.

**Use:** obtain mandate and economic accountability.

**Do not rely on them alone:** they may not know the actual handoffs and source-state problems.

---

### B. End-to-end operational owner — primary entry door

Common titles:
- Head/Manager of Order Fulfilment
- Distribution Manager
- Supply Chain Operations Manager
- Network Operations Manager
- Control Tower Lead/Manager
- Customer Service & Logistics Manager
- Regional Operations Manager

Owns:
- actual customer/business outcome;
- cross-team operating performance;
- exceptions and escalation;
- daily priorities;
- handoffs;
- service KPIs.

**This is the preferred first door.**

Why:
SynapseCore creates value only if the person responsible for the outcome recognizes the need. This role can explain what happens between systems.

---

### C. Operational champion / control-room user

Common titles:
- Control Tower Controller
- Operations Controller
- Fulfilment Manager/Controller
- Logistics Controller
- DC Operations Manager
- Supply Chain Analyst
- Distribution Planning/Analytics Manager

Owns:
- live monitoring;
- exception handling;
- data reconciliation;
- coordination;
- root-cause analysis;
- escalation;
- operational follow-through.

**Use:** obtain the real day-in-operation truth.

This person is often the strongest validator of whether SynapseCore saves meaningful investigation/coordination effort.

---

### D. Planning / intelligence owner

Common titles:
- S&OP Manager
- IBP Manager/Planner
- Demand Planning Manager
- Supply Planning Manager
- Network Planning Manager
- Supply Chain Analytics Manager

Owns:
- demand/supply alignment;
- forecast and planning assumptions;
- scenarios;
- future constraints;
- cross-functional planning cadence.

**Use:** map how future-oriented intelligence feeds current operational decisions.

**Important:** do not confuse S&OP/IBP with realtime operational control.

---

### E. Systems/data owner — technical gatekeeper and co-owner

Common titles:
- Customer Service & Logistics Systems Manager
- Supply Chain Systems Manager
- Business Systems Manager
- ERP/WMS/TMS Product Owner
- Supply Chain Technology Manager
- Enterprise/Integration Architect
- Data/BI Lead

Owns:
- systems of record;
- integration architecture;
- data quality;
- platform ownership;
- access/security constraints;
- source APIs;
- stability/scalability.

**Use:** confirm technical feasibility and protect source authority.

**Commercial rule:** bring them early, but do not make them responsible for inventing the business case.

---

### F. Domain authority owners

Depending on operating model:
- Procurement / Buying
- Warehouse / DC
- Production
- Quality
- Transport
- Customer Service
- Sales/Commercial
- Inventory
- Finance

They retain authority over specialist decisions and source systems.

**SynapseCore must coordinate context without pretending to own every domain.**

## 4. The correct founder entry sequence

### First contact
Ask for the person responsible for **end-to-end operational fulfilment/distribution/customer service performance** for the relevant business unit or region.

Do not ask:
“Who manages your WMS?”

Ask:
“Who is responsible when an order/customer commitment crosses stock, warehouse, sourcing and delivery and something changes?”

### First meeting
Participants ideally:
- operational sponsor/owner;
- operational champion/controller.

Objective:
reconstruct one real operating journey.

No technical demo-first approach.

### Second meeting
Add:
- systems/data owner;
- relevant domain owner(s).

Objective:
verify authoritative sources, existing capabilities, data boundaries and technical feasibility.

### Third step
Only if the value gap is real:
- executive/economic sponsor;
- IT/security;
- procurement/legal as needed.

Objective:
bounded pilot business case and safe implementation path.

## 6. New strategic market finding: internal ownership is a major competitor

The Tiger Brands evidence is especially important.

A large enterprise may:
- buy a control-tower service;
- later insource it;
- build internal system ownership;
- integrate WMS/OMS/TMS/data internally;
- operate its own analytics and exception processes.

Therefore SynapseCore competes against:
1. external control-tower vendors;
2. suite products;
3. internal engineering/systems teams;
4. managed 3PL operations;
5. existing human coordination.

This changes the sales test.

The question is not:
“Can SynapseCore do something no one else can do?”

The question is:
“Is adopting SynapseCore a better operational/economic decision for this customer and scope than extending what they already own?”

That must be proven customer by customer.

## 7. What a strong sponsor will care about

The sponsor is unlikely to care first about Java, Redis or WebSockets.

They will care about:
- how quickly the operation knows what matters;
- confidence in the operational picture;
- fewer unresolved exceptions;
- service/customer outcomes;
- faster decision and escalation;
- better accountability;
- less manual reconciliation;
- recovery when systems or partners fail;
- implementation burden;
- integration risk;
- measurable economics.

The technical architecture matters because it must make those claims trustworthy.

---

## 8. What a systems owner will care about

Expect:
- what systems remain authoritative;
- how data enters;
- mapping/identity;
- duplicate handling;
- latency/freshness;
- security;
- permissions;
- source writeback;
- failure/replay;
- audit;
- scale;
- vendor lock-in;
- operational support;
- how SynapseCore avoids becoming another silo.

This is where the final M6/M7 evidence becomes commercially important.

## 9. What this means for outreach

### Wrong outreach
“We built a control tower that connects ERP/WMS systems. Can we demo it?”

### Better outreach
“We are building an operational intelligence and control system for complex operations. We are researching how South African operators manage a customer/business outcome when several systems and teams have to stay aligned and conditions change. I’d like to understand one real journey in your operation and show you what we have only if it is relevant.”

This protects credibility and gives us information.
