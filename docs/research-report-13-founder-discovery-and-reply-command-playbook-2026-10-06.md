# SynapseCore Research Report 13 — Founder Discovery and Reply Command Playbook
**Date:** 2026-10-06 (Africa/Johannesburg)  
**Purpose:** Define how every meaningful company reply should be handled so outreach converts into company-confirmed operational evidence, not premature demos, generic selling or product narrowing.

---

## 1. Core rule

The first objective after a reply is **not to sell the pilot**.

It is to determine:
1. who owns the operating outcome;
2. how the company currently maintains operational truth across systems/teams;
3. where changing conditions create real cross-domain consequences;
4. how priorities/decisions are made;
5. how actions/handoffs are verified;
6. how failures/recovery are managed;
7. what is already solved well;
8. whether SynapseCore would add material incremental value.

If that case does not survive discovery, do not force the opportunity.

---

## 2. Evidence classification during discovery

Every statement learned from a company must be classified as one of:

- **PUBLIC VERIFIED FACT** — already supported by public source.
- **COMPANY-CONFIRMED FACT** — explicitly stated by the company in email/meeting.
- **OBSERVED FACT** — directly shown in a process/system walkthrough.
- **HYPOTHESIS** — plausible but unconfirmed.
- **SYNAPSCORE CURRENT CAPABILITY** — exists now in accepted implementation scope.
- **INTENDED M6 CONTRACT** — required complete engine behavior but not necessarily currently pilot-authorized.
- **FUTURE / OUT OF SCOPE** — not part of the current accepted product.

Never silently promote a hypothesis into a customer fact.

---

## 3. Reply class A — "Yes / let's talk"

### Immediate response
Accept the conversation and keep the first meeting to approximately 20–30 minutes.

Request only what is needed:
- appropriate operational owner;
- one operator/controller if available;
- no large technical attendance requirement yet.

### Meeting objective
Reconstruct one real current operating situation that crosses more than one team/system/domain.

Do not start with a product tour.

### Opening sequence
1. Thank them and confirm why the company was selected.
2. Re-state SynapseCore in one short whole-product description.
3. Say the purpose is to understand their existing operating model before judging fit.
4. Ask them to choose a real recent or representative situation.

### Primary discovery questions
- What operational outcome are you ultimately responsible for?
- Which teams/systems contribute to that outcome?
- When conditions change, how do you know what changed and what else is affected?
- How is priority decided when several important things compete?
- Who has authority to make each class of decision?
- How are actions handed off and followed through?
- How do you know the expected outcome actually occurred?
- What happens when a source system, integration, external partner or internal process fails?
- What is already working very well today?
- Where does manual coordination/reconciliation still occur, if anywhere?
- Which metric would improve if the control problem were materially better?

### End condition
Do not promise a pilot.

State one of:
- no clear fit yet;
- more operational discovery needed;
- systems/data discovery justified;
- potential bounded pilot candidate subject to technical and commercial gates.

---

## 4. Reply class B — referral to another person

This is a positive routing signal, not market validation.

### Action
- thank the referrer;
- contact the referred person in the same thread where possible;
- explain SynapseCore fully rather than assuming context transferred;
- state why the referrer directed us to them;
- ask for operational discovery.

### Record
Update outreach log with:
- referrer;
- referred person/title;
- reason for referral if given;
- whether the referral is operational, technical, procurement or executive.

---

## 5. Reply class C — "Send more information"

Do not send a long deck by default.

### Send a concise founder brief containing
1. What SynapseCore is.
2. What it is not.
3. Complete operational loop.
4. Why their company was contacted.
5. Typical problem structures without claiming they have them.
6. Human authority/source-system boundaries.
7. Current status: preparing for controlled South African pilots; technical launch remains gated.
8. Request for a short discovery conversation.

### Do not send
- invented ROI;
- generic 30-page deck;
- architecture dump;
- unsupported customer logos;
- claims of autonomous execution;
- current implementation details presented as complete enterprise coverage.

---

## 6. Reply class D — "Can you demo it?"

A demo request is useful, but demo-first can misframe the product.

### Response
Agree conditionally:
- first spend 10–15 minutes understanding their operating environment;
- then show only the relevant SynapseCore loop;
- label current implementation vs intended accepted M6 behavior clearly.

### Demo discipline
Show:
- operational truth;
- pressure/intelligence;
- prioritisation;
- recommendations/options;
- governance/authority;
- operator decision;
- action/handoff boundary;
- outcome verification;
- audit/recovery.

Do not show disconnected pages as a feature catalogue.

---

## 7. Reply class E — "We already have ERP/WMS/TMS/MES/control tower/BI"

This is expected and can be a strong signal of maturity.

### Never respond
"SynapseCore is better."

### Ask
- Which system owns which truth?
- What coordinates consequences across those systems?
- How are priorities managed across domains?
- What happens when source systems disagree or update at different times?
- How are decisions and handoffs tracked through verified outcome?
- How is recovery handled when integrations or dependencies fail?
- Is there already a control layer that does all of this well?

### Decision
If their current environment already closes the whole loop sufficiently, disqualify rather than manufacture a gap.

If a specific incremental gap remains, document it precisely.

---

## 8. Reply class F — "What does it integrate with?"

Do not claim universal prebuilt integrations.

### Current-safe answer
SynapseCore's integration architecture accepts supported inbound lanes such as APIs/webhooks, scheduled pulls and file-based ingestion, with source-specific mapping/normalisation into canonical operational models. Existing source systems retain authority unless an approved direct-operation capability exists.

### Then ask
- Which systems would be relevant to the operating outcome?
- What APIs/webhooks/files/events are available?
- Which source is authoritative for each fact?
- What latency/freshness is required?
- Which writes/actions must remain external?

Technical feasibility is a later qualification gate.

---

## 9. Reply class G — security / data concern

Treat as serious, not as sales friction.

### Explain
- tenant and role isolation are core requirements;
- source authority is preserved;
- actions require explicit authority;
- audit/recovery are product responsibilities;
- security/adversarial assurance is an explicit engineering gate;
- customer-specific M7 verification is mandatory before pilot authorization.

### Do not claim
- "bank-grade";
- "unhackable";
- completed M4 execution;
- compliance certifications not actually held.

### Next step
Bring the systems/security owner into a dedicated session after operational relevance is established.

---

## 10. Reply class H — pricing question

Do not quote a permanent enterprise subscription blindly.

### Answer structure
- first pilot is a supervised, bounded operational engagement;
- commercial scope depends on integration burden, operating scope, support, workload and measurement;
- discovery establishes the scope before a formal pilot quote;
- production commercial terms are separate from pilot terms.

### If a budget range is required early
Provide a clearly labelled provisional range only after:
- company scale is understood;
- pilot boundary exists;
- systems/integration burden is estimated;
- support/hosting/resource needs are known.

Do not underprice merely to secure logo access.

---

## 11. Reply class I — "Not interested / no need"

Do not argue.

### Ask only if appropriate
A single useful question:
- is the reason timing, existing capability, scope mismatch, budget, or simply no operational need?

If they answer, record the learning.
If not, close respectfully.

A clean disqualification improves the market model.

---

## 12. Reply class J — procurement/tender route

### Action
Determine:
- whether supplier onboarding is required;
- NDA requirements;
- procurement policy;
- RFP/tender timing;
- insurance/security requirements;
- company registration/tax/B-BBEE documentation;
- technical prerequisites;
- whether discovery can occur before procurement.

Do not allow procurement mechanics to masquerade as product validation.

---

## 13. First meeting roles

Ideal first meeting:
- operational owner/sponsor;
- operational champion/operator.

Possible second meeting:
- systems/data owner;
- relevant domain owner.

Later:
- security;
- procurement/legal;
- executive/economic approver.

Do not fill the first call with technical stakeholders before the operating need is understood.

---

## 14. Company discovery record template

For every conversation record:

### Company
Name / business unit / geography.

### Participants
Name, title, operational responsibility.

### Operating outcome
What business result they own.

### Current source systems
Only company-confirmed systems.

### Current control mechanism
How they maintain shared truth, priorities and decisions today.

### Pain / opportunity
Exact company language where possible.

### Existing strengths
What already works well.

### Failure/recovery
How exceptions, dependency failures and missed handoffs are recovered.

### Authority
Who can decide/approve/act.

### Measurable baseline
Current metric and source.

### Potential SynapseCore role
Precise incremental role — or "none".

### Risks
Safety, regulation, integration, data quality, adoption, timing, procurement.

### Qualification gates
Pass / fail / unknown for Gates 1–10.

### Next action
One concrete next step.

---

## 15. Pilot proposal threshold

Do not propose a pilot until at minimum:

- Gate 1 real operational problem: PASS;
- Gate 2 sponsor: PASS;
- Gate 3 operator: PASS;
- Gate 4 source feasibility: plausible and scheduled for technical validation;
- Gate 5 bounded connected scope: PASS;
- Gate 6 measurable baseline: defined;
- Gate 8 authority/safety: no unresolved blocker;
- Gate 9 operator participation: committed;
- commercial path appears credible.

Gate 7 technical compatibility must pass through actual M6/M7 engineering acceptance before launch.

---

## 16. Follow-up timing rule

For first-contact emails:
- do not treat weekends as business-day response time;
- first follow-up normally after ~3 full business days;
- second/final follow-up approximately 7–10 business days after initial contact if still justified;
- do not mechanically chase every account;
- use a better route/person instead of repeated inbox pressure when routing is likely the problem.

A meaningful referral or acknowledgement resets the next-action clock.

---

## 17. Command principle

Every conversation must move the evidence state forward:

**public hypothesis -> company-confirmed operating truth -> quantified/observable control gap (if one exists) -> source/authority feasibility -> bounded pilot thesis -> engineering/commercial gate decision**

If the conversation does not improve evidence, we are merely selling noise.
