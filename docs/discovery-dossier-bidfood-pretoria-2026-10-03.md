# Founder Discovery Dossier — Bidfood South Africa / Pretoria
**Date:** 2026-10-03  
**Purpose:** Prepare a founder-level operational discovery conversation.  
**Status:** Public research only; no company-confirmed problem, sponsor or pilot.

## Why Bidfood is strategically strong
Bidfood operates a broad-line foodservice distribution model with multiple temperature regimes, a national branch network, customer orders, procurement, warehouse operations and scheduled delivery.

MyBidfood already provides strong customer-facing digital capability: live account pricing and stock availability, substitutes, back-in-stock notifications, ordering, account administration, user permissions and order tracking up to delivery.

Pretoria publicly identifies separate branch functions for General Management, Sales, Operations, Procurement and MyBidfood administration, and publishes delivery areas across Gauteng/North West.

That makes Bidfood the richest visible full-loop environment in the first wave.

## Full-loop hypothesis
**Foodservice customer demand -> live stock / substitute option -> order -> procurement/replenishment -> warehouse/cold-chain execution -> route/cutoff -> delivery -> customer outcome -> exception/recovery**

The question is whether the branch already has a sufficiently integrated operating-control environment across those functions, and if not, whether SynapseCore can add value without interfering with food-safety or specialist execution controls.

## What we need to learn
- ERP/WMS/TMS and source ownership.
- How MyBidfood stock availability is generated.
- What happens when stock changes after order intent.
- Substitution rules and authorization.
- Procurement response to shortages.
- Delivery cutoff / route consequence handling.
- Branch versus national decision authority.
- Existing control/exception dashboards.
- Food-safety/quality boundaries that SynapseCore must never override.
- Data-access, privacy and security constraints.
- Whether one branch can be isolated for a meaningful pilot.

## Founder opening
Ask to understand one normal customer-order journey that becomes operationally difficult after expected stock, substitution or delivery circumstances change.

The purpose is to learn how sales, procurement, operations and the digital-order environment stay aligned.

## Eight high-value discovery questions
1. When an ordered item becomes unavailable or constrained, what does the branch do from detection through customer resolution?
2. How are substitute, replenishment and delivery implications compared?
3. Which system is authoritative for stock that is actually fulfillable?
4. Who owns the customer commitment while procurement, warehouse and delivery actions are pending?
5. Which decisions are local and which require national/head-office authority?
6. Where is the decision and follow-through visible across teams?
7. What happens when live stock or an integration is stale?
8. Which food-safety, quality or warehouse controls must remain completely outside any new control layer?

## Evidence not to claim
- Do not imply MyBidfood stock is inaccurate.
- Do not imply cold-chain or safety problems.
- Do not promise substitution/procurement/transport automation.
- Do not claim Bidfood lacks an internal control system.
- Do not use food-safety risk as a sales scare tactic.

## Potential bounded pilot shape
One branch (Pretoria is a practical research candidate) + selected customer/order/delivery territories + a non-safety-critical decision scope spanning sales, procurement and operations.

The value test should be cross-functional coordination and verified customer outcome, not warehouse execution replacement.

## Useful baseline metrics if company confirms relevance
- stock-exception to customer-decision time;
- substitution/replenishment decision latency;
- manual cross-team handoffs;
- customer status-update latency;
- unresolved orders near route cutoff;
- decision-to-confirmed fulfillment/delivery;
- source freshness and recovery;
- operator investigation time.

## Disqualifiers for the proposed scope
- Existing branch/national control process already provides a strong integrated loop.
- Required source access cannot be safely approved.
- Only meaningful value requires food-safety-critical or autonomous operational authority.
- No branch-level sponsor or ability to isolate scope.
