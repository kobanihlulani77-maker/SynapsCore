# SynapseCore Research Report 17 — Control-Tower Comparison and Discovery Test
**Date:** 2026-10-07 (Africa/Johannesburg)
**Mode:** JARVIS / responsible-partner execution
**Purpose:** Compare serious South African/global control-tower operating models against the complete SynapseCore control loop without reducing competitors to dashboards or overstating SynapseCore's current integration breadth.

---

## 1. Executive conclusion

Control towers already solve substantial parts of the problem SynapseCore addresses.

Serious control-tower offerings can already provide:
- multi-source data integration;
- centralised operational visibility;
- real-time exception management;
- trained control teams;
- predictive insight;
- decision rules;
- transport/order/PO management;
- performance governance;
- measurable service/cost outcomes.

Therefore SynapseCore must **not** position itself around:
- 'one dashboard';
- 'one source of truth' as a generic claim;
- real-time visibility alone;
- exception alerts alone;
- recommendations alone;
- 'your ERP/WMS is fragmented' as a blanket pitch.

The commercial question is whether SynapseCore can provide **incremental whole-operation control** in a chosen scope by combining the relevant supported operational domains with explicit authority/governance, operator control, outcome reconciliation, audit and recovery — at a better fit/economic/operating burden than extending what the company already has.

If a prospect already has that whole loop adequately covered, do not manufacture a gap.

## 2. Benchmark: Transnova / Tiger Brands

Public Transnova material describes a mature Logistics Control Tower built with Tiger Brands. It is not a passive dashboard.

Verified/vendor-reported characteristics include:
- centralised order and transport planning;
- multi-carrier procurement/performance management;
- real-time visibility across 60+ carriers;
- predictive ETA and exception management;
- TMS workflow integration;
- BI and advanced analytics;
- a jointly staffed operating model;
- performance governance;
- a performance-aligned commercial model;
- material, vendor/customer-reported logistics savings and service improvements.

Sources:
- https://www.transnova.co.za/solutions/logistics-control-tower
- https://transnova.co.za/2025/12/05/tiger-brands-and-transnova-win-gold-awardat-the-2025-logistics-achiever-awards/
- https://transnova.co.za/2026/07/20/transnova-expanded-as-tiger-brands-lead-logistics-partner-after-open-tender/
- https://transnova.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Case_Study_-_Tiger_Brands_-_Logistics_Control_Tower.pdf

### What this proves

A buyer can already purchase a serious operational-control service that combines people, process, technology, rules, analytics and logistics execution management.

### Where SynapseCore must test incremental value

Not 'better visibility'. Instead ask whether the company needs a control system that extends beyond the logistics/transport mandate into the connected operational state SynapseCore actually supports in the pilot: order, inventory/reservation, fulfillment, integration health/freshness, operational intelligence, operator governance, outcome readback and replay/recovery.

Do not imply Transnova lacks governance, action, analytics or outcome measurement; public evidence shows strong capability there.

## 3. Benchmark: DSV Supply Chain Management / control towers

DSV publicly markets specialised control towers that:
- integrate multiple stakeholders into a collaborative platform;
- oversee flows from order receipt to delivery;
- manage exceptions/escalations;
- provide PO and SKU/part-level status;
- support predictive insights;
- manage multi-LSP environments;
- connect/integrate with customer systems;
- provide dashboards, analytics and operational specialists.

Sources:
- https://www.dsv.com/en-za/digital-solutions/supply-chain-management
- https://www.dsv.com/en-za/insights/expert-opinions/control-towers

### Implication

Again, 'end-to-end', 'predictive', 'exception management' and 'integration' are not unique claims.

The discovery question is about **control boundary**:
- Which domains are actually represented?
- Which facts are authoritative?
- Which operator owns cross-domain decisions?
- Which actions are controlled directly?
- How is completion/outcome verified?
- How are source/integration failures recovered?
- How far outside logistics/PO/transport does the operating model reach?

## 4. Food Lover's Market — existing control-tower initiative

A public Food Lover's Market Control Tower Specialist role reported to the National Logistics Manager and described:
- building a control-tower platform;
- end-to-end visibility of shipments, inventory and supply-chain flows;
- disruption detection/escalation;
- coordination across procurement, warehouse, customer service, carriers, suppliers and 3PLs;
- trend/bottleneck analysis;
- incident/crisis support;
- event/exception documentation.

Source:
- https://za.linkedin.com/in/edwin-deyzel-69639b20b

### Important evidence classification

This is evidence of **intent/role design and control-tower work**, not proof from public sources that the final platform is fully deployed, what product it uses, or its exact current scope.

### Discovery test

We must ask:
1. Is the control tower live today or still evolving?
2. What systems/data feed it?
3. Is it logistics-only, broader supply chain, or cross-company operations?
4. Does it maintain order + inventory + fulfillment operational state or primarily shipment/logistics state?
5. How are priorities/recommendations generated?
6. Who has decision authority?
7. Are actions tracked through confirmed business outcome?
8. What happens when an integration/source feed fails?
9. Is recovery/replay built into the operating model?
10. What meaningful gap, if any, remains that SynapseCore could address?

Do not pitch SynapseCore as 'another control tower'.

### Implication

Shoprite is not a target because its technology is weak. It is a strategic stress test of the SynapseCore proposition.

Famous Brands publicly defines Supply Chain across Manufacturing, Logistics and Retail. Current priorities include:

Sources:
- https://famousbrands.co.za/supply-chain/

### Discovery test

The company already has integrated ownership and strong specialist systems.

The key questions are:
- Is there one operational control model across Manufacturing + Logistics + franchise/retail demand, or mainly strong domain systems?
- How are cross-domain priorities governed?
- How are decisions handed off and confirmed?
- What happens when one domain's state changes the consequence in another?
- How are integration failures and stale state surfaced/recovered?

Again, do not assume a gap because Famous Brands wants more value from data.

## 7. Comparison dimensions that matter

| Dimension | Serious logistics/control-tower benchmark | SynapseCore pilot question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Source integration | Often broad across logistics/PO/carriers/partners | Can required authoritative facts across supported operational domains reach SynapseCore? |
| Visibility | Mature real-time/end-to-end claims common | Does SynapseCore create a materially more useful connected operational state? |
| Exception management | Common | Does SynapseCore improve condition/pressure/prioritisation and operator response? |
| Predictive/analytics | Common | Are SynapseCore recommendations/intelligence better fitted to the chosen operating decision? |
| Human operations | Managed teams/operators common | Are roles/authority/governance clearer or better aligned to company ownership? |
| Action/execution | Some towers actively manage transport/PO actions | SynapseCore must remain honest about supported actions vs external handoff |
| Outcome verification | Often delivery/performance tracking | Does authoritative readback close the decision-to-outcome loop for the chosen scope? |
| Integration failure recovery | Varies publicly | SynapseCore's replay/failure evidence/recovery can matter if it solves a real operational burden |
| Domain breadth | Often supply-chain/logistics centred, but can be broad | Is the prospect's needed control picture broader than the incumbent scope and within SynapseCore's supported model? |
| Governance/audit | Often present in mature services | Test whether SynapseCore's explicit role/approval/audit model is incrementally useful |
| Commercial model | Managed service, platform/service, performance aligned | Can SynapseCore deliver equal/better value with acceptable implementation/support burden? |

## 8. SynapseCore differentiation discipline

Do **not** claim any of the following without company-specific proof:
- competitors are only dashboards;
- incumbents cannot prioritise;
- incumbents cannot recommend;
- incumbents cannot integrate multiple systems;
- incumbents do not have human control;
- incumbents do not measure outcomes.

Potential SynapseCore incremental value must instead be established empirically around:
- cross-domain operating truth for the chosen scope;
- explicit authority/governed decisions;
- connected recommendations and operator control;
- authoritative outcome readback;
- visible integration failure/replay/recovery;
- auditability and tenant/role isolation;
- implementation burden and economics;
- ability to sit above existing sources without replacing them.

These are **test dimensions**, not guaranteed unique advantages.

## 9. Discovery scorecard for control-tower-capable prospects

For Food Lover's, Shoprite, Famous Brands and any similar account, record:

1. **Current control scope** — logistics, supply chain, or wider operations?
2. **Domains represented** — orders, demand, inventory, fulfillment, procurement, production, transport, partners, customer state?
3. **Source authority** — where does each fact come from?
4. **Freshness** — what is real-time, near-real-time, batch?
5. **Cross-domain synthesis** — does one state affect priorities in another domain?
6. **Decision model** — alerts only, recommendations, decision rules, optimization, human judgement?
7. **Authority/governance** — who can decide, approve, escalate?
8. **Action path** — does the tower execute, orchestrate, or hand off?
9. **Outcome closure** — how is completion/result observed?
10. **Failure/recovery** — what happens when source/integration/control technology fails?
11. **Audit** — can the company reconstruct who knew/decided/did what and why?
12. **Economics** — what people/system/service cost is required to operate it?

Only after this comparison can we say whether SynapseCore is complementary, competitive, unnecessary, or requires future product expansion.

## 10. Commercial conclusion

The market is validating that companies pay for **operational control**, not merely software screens.

That strengthens the market thesis but raises the bar:

> SynapseCore must demonstrate that its complete connected control loop produces a better operational/economic result for the selected scope than the customer's existing control model or the alternatives they could buy.

That is the standard for discovery, pilot qualification and eventual pricing.
